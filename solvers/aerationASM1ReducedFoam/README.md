# aerationASM1ReducedFoam

`aerationASM1ReducedFoam` is a custom OpenFOAM v2412 solver for
steady three-dimensional transport and reaction of a reduced
ASM1-derived aerobic carbon-removal model.

The solver operates on developed, time-averaged hydrodynamic and
oxygen-transfer fields generated by a preceding transient Euler–Euler
aeration simulation.

It is the second stage of the project workflow and uses fields prepared
from [`aerationDOFoam`](../aerationDOFoam/README.md). The solver does not
rerun the transient multiphase calculation.

## 1. Purpose and modelling scope

The solver calculates how a developed aeration and circulation field
affects steady dissolved-oxygen and biodegradable-substrate distributions.

The modelling sequence is:

```text
transient Euler-Euler aeration CFD
             ↓
developed time-averaged flow and oxygen-transfer fields
             ↓
aerationASM1ReducedFoam
             ↓
steady spatial distributions of SS, XS and SO
```

It is an ASM1-derived aerobic carbon subset, not a complete
wastewater-treatment-plant model.


## 2. Scientific basis

Activated Sludge Model No. 1 (ASM1) represents wastewater components
as biologically meaningful state variables connected through a
stoichiometric process matrix.

Gujer and Henze (1991) describe the mathematical construction of ASM1
and the use of separate soluble and particulate substrate fractions,
heterotrophic biomass, oxygen limitation, hydrolysis, growth, decay,
and nitrogen processes.

The present solver retains the ASM1 processes needed for aerobic carbon
removal. Smets et al. (2003) describe an ASM1 reduction for a different
objective; the reduction implemented here is specific to this project.

Sánchez et al. (2018) provide an example of CFD coupling between reactor
hydrodynamics and ASM1 biological processes.

## 3. Custom implementation

Standard OpenFOAM transport solvers can advect and diffuse scalar
fields, but the present study additionally requires:

- coupled nonlinear ASM1-derived reaction rates;
- simultaneous transport of `SS`, `XS`, and `SO`;
- local aerobic heterotrophic growth;
- aerobic hydrolysis of slowly biodegradable substrate;
- stoichiometric oxygen consumption;
- coupling to spatially varying oxygen-transfer capacity;
- coupling to spatially varying oxygen saturation;
- use of frozen time-averaged multiphase hydrodynamics;
- construction of a conservative biological water flux; and
- biological diagnostic fields for verification and post-processing.

These coupled operations are implemented in `aerationASM1ReducedFoam`.


## 4. Relationship to full ASM1

The following ASM1 organic and oxygen variables are represented in the reduced case files:

| Field | Meaning | Current treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `SI` | Soluble inert organic matter | Prescribed/carried |
| `SS` | Readily biodegradable substrate | Actively solved |
| `XI` | Particulate inert organic matter | Prescribed/carried |
| `XS` | Slowly biodegradable substrate | Actively solved |
| `XBH` | Active heterotrophic biomass | Prescribed |
| `XP` | Particulate products | Retained/reserved |
| `SO` | Dissolved oxygen | Actively solved |

The active biological processes are:

1. aerobic heterotrophic growth;
2. aerobic hydrolysis of `XS` to `SS`; and
3. heterotrophic oxygen consumption.

The model does not currently include:

- anoxic heterotrophic growth;
- denitrification;
- autotrophic growth;
- nitrification;
- dynamic heterotrophic biomass growth and washout;
- biomass decay;
- dynamic production of `XP`;
- secondary clarification;
- return activated sludge;
- waste activated sludge;
- solids-retention-time dynamics; or
- plant-wide recycle streams.

`bH`, `fP`, and `XP` remain in the configuration and source structure,
but decay and particulate decay-product formation are inactive because
`XBH` is prescribed rather than dynamically balanced.


## 5. Frozen hydrodynamic coupling

The biological solver uses fields prepared after the transient
air–water simulation reaches its developed period:

Their roles in the reduced model are:

| Field | Use in the biological stage |
|---|---|
| `alphaUwaterMean` | Starting point for the conservative biological face flux |
| `alpha.waterMean` | Mean liquid fraction and liquid-region weighting |
| `nut.waterMean` | Turbulent scalar diffusivity |
| `DOMean` | Initial/reference mean aeration DO field; not the final ASM1 `SO` solution |
| `DOsatLocalMean` | Spatially varying, pressure-dependent mean oxygen-saturation field |
| `oxygenTransferCoeffPostMean` | Derived mean bubble oxygen-transfer coefficient before atmosphere masking |

They are time-averaged and derived coupling fields and are not all
direct outputs of `aerationDOFoam`. For the present study, the developed
aeration averaging period is **180–300 s**.

The biological calculation is consequently one-way coupled:

```text
aeration hydrodynamics → biological response
```

Biological reactions do not feed back into gas holdup, turbulence,
bubble motion, or liquid momentum.


## 6. Conservative biological flow field

Direct time averaging of a transient multiphase velocity field does not
guarantee that the resulting face flux is exactly divergence free.

Using a non-conservative averaged flux in a steady scalar calculation
could therefore introduce artificial substrate accumulation or removal.

The solver constructs a dedicated conservative biological flux
`alphaPhiBio`.

The procedure is:

1. construct a raw face flux from `alphaUwaterMean`;
2. set flux through the atmosphere, side walls, floor, and diffuser
   patches to zero;
3. preserve the inlet volumetric flow;
4. rescale the outlet flow to match the inlet magnitude;
5. solve a Poisson correction for the residual divergence; and
6. subtract the correction flux from the raw field.

The conservative-flux construction requires boundary patches named
`inlet`, `outlet`, `atmosphere`, `sideWalls`, `floor`, and `diffusers`.


## 7. Governing biological equations

### 7.1 Oxygen limitation

Aerobic reaction rates use the ASM1 Monod oxygen limitation:

$$
f_O =
\frac{S_O}
{K_{OH}+S_O}
$$


### 7.2 Substrate limitation

Heterotrophic growth uses

$$
f_S =
\frac{S_S}
{K_S + S_S}.
$$


### 7.3 Aerobic heterotrophic growth

The retained heterotrophic growth rate is

$$
\rho_H =
\mu_H
\frac{S_S}{K_S + S_S}
\frac{S_O}{K_{OH} + S_O}
X_{BH}.
$$

`XBH` is a prescribed retained biomass concentration rather than a
dynamically solved biomass inventory.


### 7.4 Readily biodegradable substrate consumption

The corresponding consumption of `SS` is

$$
r_{SS,\mathrm{growth}}
=
\frac{1}{Y_H}\rho_H.
$$


### 7.5 Heterotrophic oxygen consumption

The oxygen requirement associated with aerobic heterotrophic growth is

$$
r_{O_2,H}
=
\frac{1-Y_H}{Y_H}\rho_H.
$$

This is the ASM1 aerobic heterotrophic-growth oxygen stoichiometry.


### 7.6 Aerobic hydrolysis

The retained aerobic hydrolysis rate is

$$
\rho_{\mathrm{hyd}}
=
k_h
\frac{S_O}{K_{OH} + S_O}
X_{BH}
\frac{S_X}
{K_X X_{BH} + S_X}.
$$

Hydrolysis converts slowly biodegradable substrate `XS` into readily
biodegradable substrate `SS` on a COD basis.

Therefore:

$$
XS \rightarrow SS
$$

without creating or destroying biodegradable COD through the
hydrolysis conversion itself.


## 8. Scalar transport equations

The actively solved scalar equations have the general steady form

$$
\nabla\cdot
\left(
\alpha\Phi_{\mathrm{bio}} C
\right)
-
\nabla\cdot
\left(
\alpha_w D_{\mathrm{eff}}\nabla C
\right)
=
R_C.
$$

For soluble fields:

$$
D_{\mathrm{eff,sol}}
=
D_{m,\mathrm{sol}}
+
\frac{\nu_{t,w}^{\mathrm{mean}}}{Sc_T}.
$$

For particulate/suspended fields:

$$
D_{\mathrm{eff,part}}
=
D_{m,\mathrm{part}}
+
\frac{\nu_{t,w}^{\mathrm{mean}}}{Sc_T}.
$$


### 8.1 Slowly biodegradable substrate

`XS` is transported and removed by aerobic hydrolysis:

$$
R_{XS}
=
-\alpha_w\rho_{\mathrm{hyd}}.
$$


### 8.2 Readily biodegradable substrate

`SS` receives hydrolysed substrate and is consumed by heterotrophic
growth:

$$
R_{SS}
=
\alpha_w
\left(
\rho_{\mathrm{hyd}}
-
\frac{\rho_H}{Y_H}
\right).
$$


### 8.3 Dissolved oxygen

The dissolved-oxygen equation contains both biological oxygen
consumption and bubble-mediated oxygen transfer:

$$
R_{SO}
=
k_La_{\mathrm{bio}}
\left(
S_O^* - S_O
\right)
-
\alpha_w
\frac{1-Y_H}{Y_H}
\rho_H.
$$

Here `DOsatLocalMean` provides $S_O^*$, while
`oxygenTransferCoeffBio` supplies the local time-averaged
bubble-transfer coefficient.


## 9. Oxygen-transfer treatment

The transfer term in Section 8.3 gives:

```text
SO < SO*   → positive oxygenation
SO = SO*   → zero net transfer
SO > SO*   → negative transfer / stripping
```

Cells immediately adjacent to the resolved `atmosphere` patch are
excluded from the bubble-mediated coefficient so the free-surface
interface is not treated as a dispersed bubble plume. The mask is tied
to the atmosphere boundary rather than a gas-volume-fraction threshold.

Direct atmospheric free-surface reaeration is not included.


## 10. Model parameters

The current biological parameter file contains:

| Parameter | Value | Units | Role |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `DmSoluble` | $1.0\times10^{-9}$ | m²/s | Soluble molecular diffusivity |
| `DmParticulate` | $1.0\times10^{-12}$ | m²/s | Small particulate diffusivity |
| `ScT` | 0.70 | – | Turbulent Schmidt number |
| `muH` | $3.89568\times10^{-5}$ | s⁻¹ | Heterotrophic growth rate |
| `bH` | $2.72241\times10^{-6}$ | s⁻¹ | Decay parameter, inactive |
| `kh` | $3.11242\times10^{-5}$ | s⁻¹ | Hydrolysis rate |
| `KS` | 0.010 | kg COD/m³ | Substrate half-saturation |
| `KOH` | 0.0002 | kg O₂/m³ | Oxygen half-saturation |
| `KX` | 0.10 | – | Hydrolysis saturation coefficient |
| `YH` | 0.67 | – | Heterotrophic yield |
| `fP` | 0.08 | – | Decay-product fraction, inactive |

The kinetic-rate values used in the simulations correspond to the
12 °C parameterisation adopted for this study.

Their equivalent daily values are approximately:

```text
muH = 3.366 d-1
bH  = 0.235 d-1   [inactive]
kh  = 2.689 d-1
```

The parameter set is based on conventional ASM1/BSM1 benchmark-scale
values rather than calibration to biological measurements from a
specific treatment plant.


## 11. Biomass treatment

The retained heterotrophic biomass concentration is prescribed as

$$
X_{BH} = 2.55\ \mathrm{kg\,COD\,m^{-3}}
$$

for the current simulations.

This is a benchmark-scale input, not a site-specific measurement.

A fixed biomass inventory is used because the computational domain does
not contain the secondary clarifier, return-sludge stream, waste-sludge
stream, or dynamic solids-retention-time model required to predict the
long-term biomass concentration consistently.

Consequently, the solver does not predict biomass yield, sludge age,
waste-sludge production, or clarifier performance.


## 12. COD diagnostic fields

The solver writes the following COD quantities.

### Biodegradable substrate COD

$$
\mathrm{substrateCOD} = S_S + X_S
$$

This is the biodegradable-substrate diagnostic used in the
diffuser-layout comparison.


### Soluble COD

$$
\mathrm{solubleCOD} = S_I + S_S.
$$


### Mixed-liquor COD

$$
\mathrm{mixedLiquorCOD}
=
S_I + S_S + X_I + X_S + X_{BH} + X_P.
$$

`mixedLiquorCOD` must not be interpreted as final plant effluent COD.

The CFD domain contains no secondary clarifier and therefore does not
simulate separation of biomass and suspended particulate matter from
the treated liquid.


### Additional diagnostic outputs

In addition to the updated ASM1 variables and COD diagnostics, the solver
writes fields used to check the coupling and reaction calculation:

- `alphaBio`, `alphaPhiRaw`, `alphaPhiBio`, and `divAlphaPhiBio`;
- `bubbleOTMask` and `oxygenTransferCoeffBio`;
- `oxygenFactor`;
- `rhoGrowth`, `rhoHydrolysis`, and `oxygenUptakeH`.


## 13. Numerical solution

The nonlinear reaction terms depend on the current `SS`, `XS`, and
`SO` fields.

They are solved using Picard iteration.

The production settings are:

```text
maximum nonlinear correctors = 500
nonlinear tolerance          = 1e-6
relaxation                   = 1.0
```

Each scalar transport equation is linearised using the current iterate.

The biological solution represents a steady state on the frozen mean
hydrodynamic field.

Although the OpenFOAM case advances from time directory `300` to `301`,
this does not represent one physical second of biological treatment.

`300` contains the input state and frozen aeration fields.

`301` is used as the output label for the converged steady reduced-ASM1
solution.


## 14. Verification methodology

The solver implementation was verified using a deliberately limited set
of mathematical and numerical checks.

The verification included:

1. comparison of the implemented growth and hydrolysis expressions with
   the retained ASM1 process equations;
2. verification of reaction stoichiometry and source-term signs;
3. an independent point-rate calculation;
4. limiting-behaviour checks;
5. positivity and boundedness of the converged production fields;
6. nonlinear convergence of the coupled reaction system;
7. conservation of the projected biological hydraulic flux;
8. an advective/source oxygen-balance consistency check; and
9. sensitivity to tightening the nonlinear convergence tolerance.

## 15. Verification results

### 15.1 Independent reaction-rate check

A point calculation was performed using

```text
SS  = 0.0695  kg COD/m3
XS  = 0.20232 kg COD/m3
XBH = 2.55    kg COD/m3
SO  = 0.002   kg O2/m3
```

The independently calculated factors were approximately

```text
substrate limitation fS = 0.874214
oxygen limitation    fO = 0.909091
```

with process rates

```text
rhoGrowth       = 7.89493e-05 kg COD/m3/s
rhoHydrolysis   = 3.19201e-05 kg COD/m3/s
SS growth sink  = 1.17835e-04 kg COD/m3/s
oxygen uptake   = 3.88855e-05 kg O2/m3/s
```

The coded expressions reproduced the same mathematical relationships.


### 15.2 Limiting behaviour

The reaction model gives the expected limits:

| Condition | Expected response | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `SO = 0` | Aerobic growth = 0 | PASS |
| `SO = 0` | Aerobic hydrolysis = 0 | PASS |
| `SO = 0` | Heterotrophic O₂ uptake = 0 | PASS |
| `SS = 0` | Heterotrophic growth = 0 | PASS |
| `SS = 0` | Hydrolysis may continue if `XS > 0` | PASS |
| `XS = 0` | Hydrolysis = 0 | PASS |
| Increasing `SO` | Oxygen limitation approaches unity | PASS |
| Increasing `SS` | Substrate limitation approaches unity | PASS |


### 15.3 Conservative hydraulic flux

For Case A the corrected biological flow field gave

```text
inlet  = -0.0220077 m3/s
outlet =  0.0220077 m3/s
```

with projected divergence of approximately

```text
-6.27e-13 to 3.05e-11 s^-1.
```

The biological transport field therefore conserves the imposed
volumetric flow to the reported numerical precision.


### 15.4 Nonlinear convergence

Using the production tolerance of

$$
10^{-6},
$$

Case A converged after 13 nonlinear correctors.

The final maximum relative change was

$$
4.19\times10^{-7},
$$

below the prescribed convergence criterion.

The individual scalar linear solves reached residuals of approximately
$10^{-10}$.


### 15.5 Tolerance-sensitivity check

The Case A biological calculation was repeated after tightening the
nonlinear criterion from

$$
10^{-6}
$$

to

$$
10^{-8}.
$$

The tighter solution required 16 rather than 13 nonlinear correctors.

The flow-weighted outlet values were unchanged at the reported
precision:

| Variable | Tolerance $10^{-6}$ | Tolerance $10^{-8}$ |
|---|---:|---:|
| `SS` | 0.00010643137 | 0.00010643137 |
| `XS` | 0.0050102029 | 0.0050102029 |
| `SO` | 0.011892771 | 0.011892771 |

The production tolerance of $10^{-6}$ is therefore adequate for the
reported outlet quantities.


### 15.6 Production-field boundedness

For the converged Case A solution:

```text
SS = 0.000103204 to 0.00913447 kg COD/m3
XS = 0.00485669  to 0.0541817  kg COD/m3
SO = 0.00730203  to 0.0122712  kg O2/m3
```

The actively solved internal-cell concentrations remained positive.


### 15.7 Oxygen-balance consistency

For Case A, the integrated biological diagnostics gave approximately

```text
net bubble O2 transfer = 0.00241014 kg/s
heterotrophic O2 uptake = 0.00213820 kg/s
```

The conservative water flux and flow-weighted inlet/outlet dissolved
oxygen concentrations were also evaluated.

The resulting advective-plus-source oxygen budget had a residual of
approximately 0.88% relative to the integrated bubble-transfer rate
before explicitly accounting for the boundary diffusive contribution.

This is reported as an oxygen-balance consistency check, not as a
complete closed oxygen mass balance.

## 16. Assumptions and limitations

- The active process set is limited to aerobic carbon conversion; it
  does not predict nitrogen removal or plant-wide sludge behavior.
- `XBH` is prescribed, and biomass decay is inactive; biomass inventory,
  solids retention time, and sludge production are not predicted.
- Hydrodynamic and oxygen-transfer inputs are frozen mean fields with
  one-way coupling from aeration CFD to the biological response.
- Atmosphere-adjacent cells are excluded from dispersed-bubble transfer,
  and direct free-surface reaeration is not modelled.
- `substrateCOD` is biodegradable substrate COD, not measured BOD5 or
  total final-effluent COD; no fixed BOD5 conversion is applied.


## 17. Validation status

The implementation has been mathematically and numerically verified as
described above. It has not been experimentally validated against
biological measurements from this tank and is not a validated
replacement for the full IWA ASM1 model.


## 18. Building the solver

Compile from the solver directory using:

```bash
wmake
```

Check the executable with:

```bash
which aerationASM1ReducedFoam
```

Run it from a prepared biological case with:

```bash
aerationASM1ReducedFoam
```

A biological case requires the frozen aeration fields:

```text
alphaUwaterMean
alpha.waterMean
nut.waterMean
DOMean
DOsatLocalMean
oxygenTransferCoeffPostMean
```

together with the ASM1-derived fields:

```text
SI
SS
XI
XS
XBH
XP
SO
```

and:

```text
constant/asm1Properties
```


## 19. Main source files

```text
aerationASM1ReducedFoam.C
createFields.H
ASM1Eqns.H
Make/files
Make/options
```

`aerationASM1ReducedFoam.C`
: controls the reduced steady biological solution.

`createFields.H`
: reads frozen aeration fields, biological parameters and state fields,
  constructs the oxygen-transfer mask, and generates the conservative
  biological flow field.

`ASM1Eqns.H`
: evaluates the reduced ASM1 kinetics and solves the nonlinear `XS`,
  `SS`, and `SO` transport-reaction equations.

`BODEqn.H` and `CODEqns.H` are retained development files but are not
included by the current executable. Their earlier simplified BioCOD
equations are superseded by the active reduced ASM1 implementation in
`ASM1Eqns.H`.
